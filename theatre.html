After Cordelia

A dark, discordant reimagining of Shakespeare’s King Lear commissioned by Fio Theatre and directed by Mathilde Lopez.

Fio Theatre Commission · R&D Scratch · 17 September 2026

What endures in the silence following catastrophe

Written by Samatar Elmi and directed by Mathilde Lopez, After Cordelia investigates inheritance, patriarchal power, filial refusal and the linguistic fault lines that fracture between loyalty, displacement and statehood.

Set inside the fractures of a family counselling session and driven by live drums, guitar and keyboard, the piece tests entitlement, sibling complicity and male violence—developed through two capacity 30-minute scratch performances at Chapter Arts Centre in Cardiff.

After Cordelia · Dramaturgy

Form & themes

Poetic language in conversation with the classical canon.

Cordelia’s initial utterance in Shakespeare’s tragedy—“Nothing, my lord”—is not an absence of emotion, but a refusal to commodify affection for dynastic gain. After Cordelia interrogates what happens when that refusal reverberates through the diaspora, through generational memory, and through imperial collapse.

The work combines lyrical verse, multilingual cadences, and contemporary physical dramaturgy, creating an intimate yet epic theatrical encounter that speaks directly to the political and ecological fractures of our present moment.

Core Themes

Inheritance · Refusal of flattery · Colonial and imperial legacies · Father-daughter lineages · Silence as resistance.

01 · Text & Myth

Classical Interrogation

02 · Live Instrumentation

Band & Ensemble Friction

Drawing from Elmi’s parallel work as musician Knomad Spock, live drums, guitar, keyboard and free-jazz disruption operate as active dramaturgical pressure inside the scene.
