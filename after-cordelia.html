Project overview

Logline & Commission

What endures in the silence following catastrophe.

After Cordelia is a new theatrical work reimagining Shakespeare’s King Lear. Commissioned by Fio Theatre, the play investigates inheritance, patriarchal power, filial refusal and the linguistic fault lines that fracture between loyalty, displacement and statehood.

Dramaturgy

Form & themes

Poetic language in conversation with the classical canon.

Cordelia’s initial utterance in Shakespeare’s tragedy—“Nothing, my lord”—is not an absence of emotion, but a refusal to commodify affection for dynastic gain. After Cordelia interrogates what happens when that refusal reverberates through the diaspora, through generational memory, and through imperial collapse.

The work combines lyrical verse, multilingual cadences, and contemporary physical dramaturgy, creating an intimate yet epic theatrical encounter that speaks directly to the political and ecological fractures of our present moment.

Core Themes

Inheritance · Refusal of flattery · Colonial and imperial legacies · Father-daughter lineages · Silence as resistance.
