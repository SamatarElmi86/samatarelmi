Asynchronous course · in development

Neurodiversity & the Creative Process

A self-paced programme for writers and artists who want to understand their creative patterns, work with attention and energy more deliberately, and build sustainable routes from first impulse to finished work.

Course purpose

Observe the process without forcing it into one correct shape.

The course combines reflective enquiry with practical experiments. It is strengths-aware without ignoring friction, access needs or the realities of completing work.

Recognise patterns

Map attention, sensory conditions, energy, association, initiation, transition and recovery across your own process.

Design for the work

Build flexible environments, prompts, structures and rhythms that support rather than flatten creative difference.

Move towards completion

Develop humane strategies for selecting, developing, revising, sharing and completing projects.

Proposed curriculum

Six connected modules

The final course will combine short lessons, examples from Samatar Elmi’s published writing, worksheets, quizzes and applied experiments.

  1. 01

    Your creative process

    Map beginnings, momentum, friction, stopping points, recovery and completion without moralising the pattern.

  2. 02

    Attention, sensation and energy

    Explore intensity, switching, sensory conditions, fluctuating capacity and the difference between productive immersion and depletion.

  3. 03

    Executive function and transitions

    Work with initiation, sequencing, time, working memory, interruption and the movement between imagining and making.

  4. 04

    Association and creative intelligence

    Use divergent connection, pattern, image, rhythm, memory and nonlinear thinking as deliberate creative resources.

  5. 05

    Revision, boundaries and authenticity

    Examine feedback, masking, imagined gatekeepers, ethical commitments and the conditions that allow a voice to remain recognisably yours.

  6. 06

    A sustainable practice system

    Design a personal framework for making decisions, recovering capacity and carrying work into the world.

Free taster · available now

Begin with purpose rather than productivity.

Use The Practice Compass as the first course taster. Set aside 20–30 minutes and complete three movements:

  1. Recall a moment when the work felt necessary, alive or unmistakably yours.
  2. Choose one value or non-negotiable that you want the next piece of work to embody.
  3. Design one safe, observable 30-day experiment that brings process and value into closer alignment.

Do not try to complete every question at once. The purpose is to leave with one clearer orientation and one experiment—not a perfected creative identity.

Access and boundaries

Educational, reflective and non-clinical.

No diagnosis is required to use the course.

Self-paced

Short units, printable resources and flexible pacing will allow participants to pause, repeat and approach the material in different sequences.

No compulsory disclosure

Exercises may be completed privately. Authenticity does not require unsafe exposure, disclosure or the removal of useful boundaries.

Not treatment or diagnosis

The programme does not diagnose neurodevelopmental conditions, provide therapy or replace medical, psychological or crisis support.
